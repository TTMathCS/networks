# Networks and security, from the inside out

Four books, each one course from the first idea to the questions researchers are still working on, plus fifteen labs.
Read a book in order: every chapter needs only the ones before it (and the chapters it names). Nothing is a black box:
every protocol is shown packet by packet, every number is worked out, and every claim names its source.

## How a chapter works

- **It opens with a box** saying what you will learn and which chapters you need first.
- **Every idea comes with its reason**: why a protocol is designed the way it is, what went wrong before, what the
  standard says.
- **Worked examples** are computed by hand, step by step, and checked.
- **Diagrams** show packets, paths and timing.
- **Check yourself** ends each chapter: questions with full answers folded under them. Try first, then open.
- **Sources** are in a closed box at the end: RFCs, IEEE and 3GPP standards, NIST guidance and the original papers.

## Which order?

- **New to networks:** the six [Start](../start/index.md) lessons, then *How networks work*, Parts I–III.
- **Want security:** *How networks work* chapters 1–19, then *Network security*.
- **Want radio:** *How networks work* chapters 1–7, *Math foundations* chapters 5–8, then *Wireless and mobile*.
- **Studying for a course or certificate:** each book's introduction names the courses and textbooks it follows.
